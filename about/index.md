# About

I study how AI systems shape the way young people form identity, make
decisions, and understand themselves. Specifically: when an algorithmic
system is always available to tell you what to want, watch, or believe,
what happens to the developmental work of figuring that out yourself?
I hold that question open rather than assuming the answer; whether these
systems homogenize us is something to test, not to assert.

#### Research Interests
- Youth, social media, and adolescent development
- Digital ethics, privacy, and agency
- Human-AI interaction and personalized AI agents
- Critical platform studies
- Participatory design and co-creation
- Digital behavior and individual differences (need for uniqueness, need for cognition, optimal distinctiveness)
- Autonomy and self-authorship in human-AI interaction
- Youth identity formation and participatory design

## Current Work

My research examines digital behavior through the lens of individual
differences, need for uniqueness, need for cognition, optimal
distinctiveness, with theoretical anchors in Self-Determination Theory
and Baxter Magolda's work on self-authorship. Social media is a major
empirical site, but not the only one.

Three projects are active:

An **Instagram saving behavior study** examining how personality traits
relate to what people save and why, using Big Five measures.

A **deferred trust study** testing when students defer to an AI advisor
over an institutional human one, across factual and moral dilemmas at
varying stakes.

**PACE (Personality-Agent Co-Evolution)**, a conceptual framework for the
bidirectional relationship between human personality and AI agents,
particularly during adolescence.

## What I'm Working Toward

I focus on adolescents and young adults because the identity-formation
window is when these systems have the most leverage. The design payoff
matters to me: I want this work to inform what gets built, not only what
gets published.
